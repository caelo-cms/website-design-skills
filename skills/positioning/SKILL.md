# Positioning and brand attributes

**Why:** Design follows the message. If it is unclear what the site promises and to whom, every later discussion turns into a discussion about taste. Brand attributes need an opposite pole, otherwise they fit everything: "modern" describes every website, "modern, not trendy" rules things out and becomes a test for the moodboard, the design directions and the copy.

**Needs:** `01-facts/facts.md`, `02-personas/personas.md`.
**Produces:** `03-brand/brand.md`.

If the client has an approved positioning or brand book, import it, check it against the facts and personas, and only fill the gaps.

## 1. Positioning

Work through these in order. For each, propose one recommended version and up to two alternatives, all grounded in `facts.md`:

- **Problem:** what goes wrong for the primary persona without this offer, in their words.
- **Audience:** primary and secondary persona, and who it is not for.
- **Category:** what the offer is, in terms the audience searches for. A known category is easier to find; a new one needs explaining.
- **Promise:** one sentence a visitor should remember. It must be true and provable with the fact base. Reject promises that cannot be kept (for example "before anyone else notices" when that is not technically guaranteed).
- **Pillars:** three reasons to believe the promise, each backed by facts.
- **Differentiation:** what alternatives the audience considers (competitors, doing it themselves, doing nothing) and why this offer is the better choice for the primary persona.

## 2. Brand attributes

Derive 4–5 attributes from the positioning and the personas' values. Each gets an opposite pole that names the failure mode:

| Attribute | but not |
|---|---|
| calm | dull |
| precise | cold |
| confident | loud |

Test each pair: could a design or a sentence be judged against it? If not, sharpen it.

## 3. Voice

Half a page:
- Form of address (formal or informal; in German *du* or *Sie*).
- Three rules of tone with an example sentence each, and one sentence that breaks each rule.
- Words to use and words to avoid (from `facts.md` and the personas' language).
- How numbers, product names and technical terms are written.

## Review

Show the positioning as one compact block, the attribute table and the voice rules with examples. Typical decisions: promise version, category wording, pillar order, attributes and poles, form of address.

**Done when** promise, pillars and attributes are decided. Later steps may refine wording, but changes to the promise or the attributes count as going back to this step.
