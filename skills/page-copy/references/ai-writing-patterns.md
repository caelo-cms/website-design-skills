# Typical AI writing patterns

Rewrite text that matches these. The goal is plain, specific, credible copy; the list is a check, not a style.

## Structure
- "Not just X, but Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y" framings
- Colon or dash reveals: "One thing matters: speed."
- Rule of three everywhere: three adjectives, three benefits, three short sentences in a row
- Every paragraph ending in a punchy one-liner
- Series of sentences starting with the same word ("We … We … We …")
- Rhetorical questions as section openers ("Ready to take your X to the next level?")
- Negative contrast as filler ("No fluff. No jargon. Just results.")

## Vocabulary
- Unlock, elevate, empower, supercharge, streamline, revolutionize, transform, leverage, harness
- Seamless, robust, cutting-edge, next-generation, game-changing, world-class, best-in-class
- Journey, landscape, realm, ecosystem, tapestry (when not literal)
- "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "It's worth noting"
- Stacked intensifiers: truly, incredibly, effortlessly

## Claims
- Superlatives without proof ("the best", "the leading", "#1")
- Vague social proof ("trusted by thousands", "loved by teams worldwide")
- Promises nobody can keep ("never miss anything", "100 % secure")
- Invented numbers, quotes, customer names or ratings

## Form
- Emoji as bullets or decoration
- Title Case On Every Word In Headlines (in English, unless the brand uses it)
- Scare quotes around ordinary words
- Exclamation marks in body copy

## Instead
- Say what happens, for whom, with a number or example from the fact base.
- Use the customer's words from the personas.
- Vary sentence length naturally; let some paragraphs end without a punchline.
- One claim per sentence, each one provable.
